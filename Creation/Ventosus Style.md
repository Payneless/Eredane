# The Ventosus Style: A Narrative Guide

The Ventosus style is predicated on a single, unforgiving principle: never let your opponent breathe. From the moment engagement begins, you attack. Not with the goal of landing one devastating blow, but with the understanding that each strike serves two purposes—it damages, and it creates the opening for the next strike.

Your opponent exists in constant reaction. They're reading your movements, adjusting their stance, trying to find an opening to strike back. But before they can, you're already moving again. A cut to throw off their balance. A follow-up that exploits the shift in their weight. Another strike that forces their guard into an awkward position. They never recover. They never reset. They never get to attack.

The style demands forward momentum—relentless, unyielding pressure that compounds with each exchange. It's not about finesse or reading subtle currents of movement. It's about understanding that positioning is fragile, that balance is a temporary state, and that if you disrupt it faster than someone can restore it, they cease to be a threat.

Aequorin refined it over centuries, distilling the principle into teachable sequences. The fundamentals are learnable through repetition: attack angles, balance disruption points, the rhythm of pressure that doesn't allow gaps. A student with solid combat experience and good instincts can develop competence, even without Elara's wind-touched attunement.

But here's the crucial part: the style itself carries no moral weight. It's a tool. In Elara's hands, Ventosus becomes a path to redemption—she uses that relentless pressure to disable, to render opponents unable to fight, leaving them alive for the possibility of change. The moment her opponent can no longer swing a blade, the pressure stops.

In someone else's hands? That same unyielding offense could push straight through disablement into death. The style doesn't care. It only knows one truth: maintain pressure, deny recovery, eliminate the threat.

That's what makes teaching it complicated. Elara would be giving someone an efficient killing technique while believing—perhaps foolishly—in their capacity for redemption. The wind would have guided her to them for a reason. Or so she'd tell herself.
